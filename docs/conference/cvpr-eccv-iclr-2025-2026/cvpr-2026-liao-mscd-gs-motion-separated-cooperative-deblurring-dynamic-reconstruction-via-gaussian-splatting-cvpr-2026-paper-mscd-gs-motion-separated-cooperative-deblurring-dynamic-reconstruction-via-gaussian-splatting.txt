## Abstract 

Although 4D reconstruction based on Gaussian Splat-ting has achieved many impressive results, reconstructing real-world images captured by a casual monocular camera remains a significant challenge. In dynamic scenes, as the camera and objects move during the exposure time, these input images inevitably contain a considerable amount of motion blur, which severely compromises the quality of re-construction and new view synthesis. The existing deblur-ring 3D Gaussian models still cannot directly handle mo-tion blur issues in real 4D dynamic scenes. To address these challenges, we propose MSCD-GS—a novel method for motion-separated collaborative deblurring 4D recon-struction via Gaussian Splatting, capable of effectively han-dling motion-blurred inputs. Specifically, due to the distinct motion characteristics of static and dynamic Gaussians, we perform separate motion models for dynamic scene recon-struction. To predict Gaussian changes over exposure time, we designed motion-aware networks for static and dynamic Gaussians, thereby synthesizing blurred virtual images. Fi-nally, we utilize the results from the deblurring model and the synthesized images to supervise 4D reconstruction collaboratively. Extensive experiments demonstrate that

## 1. Introduction 

4D reconstruction [13, 16, 25, 35] has emerged as an exten-sion of 3D reconstruction [9, 21], aiming not only to recover spatial geometry but also to model the temporal evolution of dynamic scenes. 4D reconstruction explicitly captures time-varying geometry, motion, and appearance changes, making it indispensable for applications such as dynamic SLAM [8, 41, 48], autonomous driving [7, 27, 50], and robotic perception [17, 18, 39]. However, the introduc-tion of the temporal dimension also brings additional chal-lenges. In dynamic scenes, both the foreground objects and the background may move relative to each other, resulting in complex non-rigid deformations and depth inconsistencies across frames. Moreover, when the camera itself is mov-ing, real-world cameras integrate light over a finite expo-sure time. Consequently, when either the camera or scene objects are in motion during this integration period, the cap-tured images inevitably contain motion blur. camera and moving objects during the exposure time. This issue frequently occurs in data acquisition. If such blurred images are used for supervised training, the model’s render-ing results will inevitably be severely compromised. Typ-ically, motion blur can be categorized into camera motion blur and object motion blur. Camera motion blur is caused by changes in camera pose during the exposure period, whereas object motion blur emerges from the intertwined movements of objects and the camera. Obviously, address-ing object motion blur is far more challenging. For the problem of camera motion blur, several NeRF-based [20, 33] and 3DGS-based [3, 47] methods generate multiple virtual frames within the exposure time to synthe-size blurred images, which are then aligned with the input blurred images. However, this type of approach only applies to deblurring static backgrounds and fails to resolve object motion blur. Currently, BARD-GS [19] and Deblur4DGS [38] are 4D reconstruction works (4D Gaussian Splatting, 4DGS) that can effectively handle both types of motion blur while maintaining real-time rendering capabilities. Never-theless, these two methods rely heavily on extensive prior data, such as depth maps, trajectories, and optical flow. Beyond the aforementioned deblurring works, signifi-cant contributions have also been made to image deblurring research [2, 4, 46] based on deep neural networks (DNNs). These methods can recover relatively sharp images using only a single blurred image, demonstrating remarkable effi-ciency and practicality. However, motion-blurred images contain numerous and complex blurred components, and the geometry information inherent in the frame is limited. Consequently, the deblurring effect inevitably yields unsat-isfactory results in terms of detail recovery, as shown in Fig-ure 1. A straightforward and concise solution is to integrate a deblurring model with 4DGS. However, relying solely on the outputs of the deblurring model to supervise 4DGS is in-feasible due to inconsistent deblurring results. The deblur-ring capability of the deblurring model will dictate the lower bound of the reconstruction performance, as illustrated in the Table 1. The issue of how to achieve the effective in-tegration of the deblurring model and 4DGS, while fully leveraging the advantages of both components, remains a challenge worthy of in-depth investigation. 

Based on the imaging principle of motion-blurred im-ages, we model the motion of objects and propose a novel deblurring 4D reconstruction framework MSCD-GS. Specifically, we utilize a deblurring model [2] to provide high-quality prior information for aiding the construction and optimization of Gaussians. Given the distinct motion characteristics of these Gaussians, we employ motion dis-tance and a motion mask to classify them into static and dy-namic Gaussians. Static Gaussians are typically rendered as the background, so camera motion blur can be repre-sented as the linear superposition of the overall motion of static Gaussians during the exposure time. We design a Static Gaussian Motion Network to predict the rigid motion of static Gaussians at multiple sampled times. In contrast, the motion of dynamic Gaussians is independent and non-linear. Therefore, we model the motion of each individual dynamic Gaussian and design a Dynamic Gaussian Motion Network to predict the motion of each dynamic Gaussian at the sampled times. Finally, we combine and render the static and dynamic Gaussians at the same sampled time to generate virtual sharp images, aligned with the prior sharp images provided by the deblurring model. Additionally, we synthesize a virtual blurred image by fusing the virtual sharp images across multiple sampled times and align this virtual blurred image with the input blurred image. This design prevents overfitting to the prior sharp images and ul-timately achieves high-quality 4D reconstruction results. In summary, our contributions are as follows: 

• By integrating a deblurring model, we propose a novel deblurring dynamic 4D scene reconstruction framework: MSCD-GS. It enables the efficient combination of deblur-ring model and dynamic reconstruction, achieving superior deblurring 4D reconstruction results. 

• We separately modeled static and dynamic Gaussian motion and constructed motion aware networks to predict Gaussian variations at different sampling times within the same exposure duration. 

• We propose a concise cooperative regularization con-straint that balances the results of the deblurring model and motion-synthesized blurred.

2.2. Reconstruction from Blurred Images 

Although several works based on NeRF [12, 20, 29, 33] and 3DGS [3, 11, 24, 47] have been proposed can render sharp images in static scenes, they still fail to reconstruct dynamic scenes. Deblur4DGS [38] achieves deblurring 4D recon-struction by estimating the exposure time and introducing a series of regularization to constrain Gaussian optimiza-tion. BARD-GS [19] decomposes motion blur and models its components separately to improve the rendering quality. Although both methods yield promising deblurring results, they both rely on multiple types of prior data to constrain model training. Although our method is similar to these two methods in that they both render multiple virtual sharp images to simulate blurred images, our method does not re-quire as much prior data for supervised training and can still achieve higher-quality 4D reconstruction. Therefore, our method is more practical. 

2.3. Images Deblurring Network 

The rise of deep learning has brought about a paradigmatic shift in image deblurring tasks, enabling models to automat-ically learn the mapping relationship between blurred and sharp images in a data-driven manner. Motion deblurring methods can be categorized by network structure into three types: CNN-based models[5, 15, 46], Transformer-based models [4, 10, 32], and diffusion-based models [14, 40, 45]. While the aforementioned methods achieve impressive de-blurring results on static images, they share a critical limi-tation: they lack 3D spatial perception. Without explicitly modeling the 3D geometry of the scene, these methods fail to maintain spatiotemporal consistency—a key requirement for video deblurring or dynamic scene processing. This leads to artifacts such as frame-wise inconsistency or in-correct depth-aware blur removal. To address this gap, our work integrates a deblurring network [2] with 4DGS, which models 3D scene geometry to enforce spatiotemporal co-herence and further boost deblurring quality.

3.2. Motion Trajectory Modeling and Deblurring 

Given a set of motion-blurred images as input to supervise the training of the Gaussian model, a blurred 4D scene will be reconstructed. Starting from the primitives that construct the scene, we decouple these primitives into static and dy-namic Gaussians. Inspired by [3, 47], we refer to the input camera poses as real camera poses, and the camera poses synthetically sampled within the exposure time as virtual camera poses. Each sampling time corresponds to a spe-cific set of motion states of Gaussians (both static and dy-namic), which will be rendered into virtual sharp images using Equation 5. Multiple virtual sharp images within the same exposure time interval are synthesized into a single motion-blurred image using Equation 1. Prior to reconstruction, we divide the input motion-blurred images into N subsets Φ = {I1 · · · IN } . We use BootsTAPIR [6, 31] to track the motion of each pixel, thereby obtaining the 2D dynamic mask MD for each im-age. Subsequently, we classify Gaussians whose 2D pro-jected centers lie within the dynamic range of the mask 

MD and whose motion distance falls within the top 1.5% as dynamic Gaussians GD , and the rest as static Gaussians GS .Within each subset, the motion of the camera and objects is continuous with small variation amplitudes, ensuring the stability of our Gaussian ellipsoid modeling. We employ a gradual reconstruction approach for each subset, and finally concatenate the subsets in chronological order to construct the entire 4D scene.

4.2. 4D Reconstruction Performance 

We evaluated the deblurring 4D reconstruction performance of MSCD-GS on the Stereo Blur dataset, as it provides blurred images along with their corresponding sharp im-ages. We took motion-blurred images as input and used the corresponding sharp images as ground truth (GT) to com-pute the PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS metrics for the rendered images. We also measure frame-per-second (FPS) for ren-dering speed and training time. The quantitative compari-son results of 4D reconstruction deblurring are shown in Ta-ble 2, and the qualitative comparison results are presented in Figure 3. Many 4D reconstruction baselines were severely affected by motion-blurred images, leading to a significant degradation in their reconstruction quality—even when as-sisted by the pre-trained deblurring model (NAFNet [2]). Although Deblur4DGS [38], BARD-GS [19], and Dy-BluRF [29] can alleviate motion blur to a certain extent, they still perform poorly in reconstructing dynamic ob-jects. In contrast, our proposed method achieves SOTA per-formance on the background or dynamic objects. Mean-while, among dynamic deblurring reconstruction methods, MSCD-GS maintains the highest rendering frame rate and the shortest training time.

4.4. Ablation 

To evaluate the impact of each module of MSCD-GS and the setting of virtual frame numbers on deblurring and novel view synthesis, we conducted experiments on Man scene of the Stereo Blur dataset. The quantitative results of the mod-ule ablation experiments are presented in Table 4, and the qualitative results are shown in Figure 5. The absence of the pre-trained deblurring network module has a relatively significant impact, as it forms the foundation for the de-blurring performance of MSCD-GS. The results in Table 1 further demonstrate that our method can efficiently inte-grate deblurring networks to achieve collaborative deblur-ring. The absence of the dynamic Gaussian deblurring mod-ule has a smaller impact due to the small size of dynamic regions in the images. The quantitative results of the vir-tual frame number experiments are provided in Table 5. As the number of virtual frames increases, the reconstruction quality metrics gradually decrease after reaching a peak. 

4.5. Limitation 

In real-world scenarios, there exist small and fast-moving objects, which are likely to appear as ghosting or even dis-appear in captured images. Since current 4D reconstruction methods rely on images for supervision, this “missing con-tent” cannot be reconstructed, as illustrated in Figure 6. One of the woman’s legs “disappears” in the image, and 4D re-construction baselines fail to infer this missing part, thus only rendering the background. In future work, a diffusion model can be introduced to generate the missing content, which then supervises the reconstruction process. 

## 5. Conclusion 

We propose MSCD-GS, a 4D Gaussian Splatting recon-struction method that integrates motion separation and a cooperative deblurring model. We separately model the motion of static and dynamic Gaussians and design two lightweight motion-aware MLPs to predict their changes during the exposure time, respectively. Additionally, a col-laborative deblurring model is used to jointly constrain the rendering results, achieving high-quality 4D reconstruction.
